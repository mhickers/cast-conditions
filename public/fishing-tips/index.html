Fishing Tips & Tactics

Catching fish is less about secret lures and more about paying attention. Where the fish are sitting, what they're eating, how the water feels that day — read those three things and you'll out-fish someone with twice your tackle.

These guides are written for anglers who are still figuring it out, and for the in-between angler who catches fish but wants to understand why. Each one tells you where a species lives, when it bites, what to throw, how to work it, and the mistakes that quietly cost beginners their first fish. Two longer pieces at the end teach you to read freshwater and saltwater conditions, which is the skill that ties everything else together.

One ground rule before you go: we never print size limits, bag limits, slot sizes, or season dates. Those change constantly and differ by state, water body, and sometimes by the week. Always check your current state regulations before you keep a fish — it's the law, and it's how the fishing stays good.

The 25 species, and why each made the list

Freshwater

1. Largemouth bass — the most-pursued gamefish in America; nearly every reader has one within reach. 2. Smallmouth bass — pound-for-pound the hardest-fighting freshwater fish, in both rivers and clear lakes nationwide. 3. Rainbow trout — the most widely stocked trout in the country, which makes it most beginners' first trout. 4. Brown trout — wary and widespread, the fish that teaches you to fish carefully. 5. Brook trout — a native jewel of cold headwater streams, and a gateway to small-stream fishing. 6. Walleye — the North's favorite fish for the table, with a devoted following and high search demand. 7. Crappie — schooling, cooperative, and delicious; arguably the best beginner gamefish there is. 8. Bluegill — the fish that hooked most of us as kids and still the best way to start a new angler. 9. Yellow perch — easy, abundant, and a year-round favorite that shines through the ice. 10. Channel catfish — found in nearly every U.S. state and willing to bite simple baits day or night. 11. Northern pike — a broad-range apex predator that gives beginners their first truly big freshwater fish (swapped in for jack crevalle to widen geographic relevance — easy to revert).

Saltwater

12. Striped bass — the iconic East Coast gamefish, fished from surf, boat, and jetty by millions. 13. Redfish (red drum) — the Gulf and Southeast's signature inshore target, reachable on foot. 14. Speckled trout — the everyman's inshore fish across the South, forgiving and abundant. 15. Fluke (summer flounder) — a Mid-Atlantic and Northeast staple with huge seasonal search demand. 16. Snook — Florida's most charismatic inshore fish and a rite of passage for new saltwater anglers. 17. Sheepshead — a structure-loving, bait-stealing puzzle that rewards anglers who learn to read the bite. 18. Black drum — redfish's bigger, easier cousin; a great confidence builder from docks and bridges. 19. Florida pompano — the surf-fishing prize of the Southeast and Gulf, caught with simple gear. 20. Black sea bass — an accessible, hard-pulling bottom fish along the entire Atlantic coast. 21. Tautog (blackfish) — the Northeast's cold-season structure specialist, a cult favorite. 22. Bluefish — aggressive, widespread, and the easiest big saltwater pull a beginner can get. 23. Kingfish (whiting) — the unsung hero of the surf: easy, tasty, and perfect for a first cast. 24. Red snapper — one of the most searched offshore species in America, with a passionate following. 25. Spanish mackerel — fast, schooling, and a thrilling light-tackle target along warm coasts.
