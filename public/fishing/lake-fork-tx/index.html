Lake Fork, TX fishing report

Lake Fork Reservoir is built for trophy largemouth bass, and serious anglers treat it accordingly. The lake's abundant standing timber, flooded creek channels, and expansive hydrilla beds give big bass exactly what they need year-round. Winter concentrates fish in deeper timber along channel edges, and this cold-water period produces some of the heaviest bass of the year on slow presentations. Spring spawning pushes fish shallow into coves and onto flats, offering sight-fishing opportunities for genuine giants. Summer bass retreat to deep hydrilla edges and submerged structure, while fall brings aggressive feeding as fish chase shad into the backs of creeks. Crappie follow similar seasonal migrations, stacking on timber in winter and moving shallow to spawn in early spring.

Work the hydrilla edges with deep-diving crankbaits or Texas-rigged plastics when water temps climb above 70 degrees. In colder water, slow down dramatically — a big swimbait or shaky head crawled through submerged timber triggers lethargic trophy fish that won't chase. For crappie, vertical jigging small curly-tail grubs or live minnows around standing timber in 15 to 25 feet is consistently productive outside of the spring spawn. Watch your depth finder for the thermocline in summer; bass suspend just above it. Creek channel bends where timber meets structure transitions are classic Fork ambush points worth spending real time on.
