Devils Lake, ND fishing report

Devils Lake is one of the Northern Plains' premier walleye and perch fisheries — a sprawling, ever-changing basin of flooded roads, timber, and bays. Walleye fishing turns on in late spring as fish move shallow to feed after the spawn, holds strong through summer on main-lake points and submerged structure, and peaks again in fall when big females fatten up before ice. Winter brings a massive hard-water fishery for jumbo perch and walleye, while northern pike prowl the weedy bays all season long.

Early and late in the day, work the shallower flats and the edges of flooded trees; as the sun climbs and the water warms, slide out to deeper points, rock piles, and the old roadbeds that crisscross the lake. Jig-and-minnow combos and bottom bouncers pulling spinner rigs with crawlers are the workhorses for walleye; downsize to small jigs tipped with minnows for perch. Pike crush spinnerbaits and large soft plastics along the weed lines. Watch the water temperature closely — a few degrees of warming pulls fish shallow and triggers a bite, while a hard cold front pushes them deep and tight-lipped.
