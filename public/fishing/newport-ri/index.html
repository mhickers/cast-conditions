Newport, RI fishing report

Newport sits where Narragansett Bay meets the open Atlantic, giving you a genuinely diverse fishery. Striped bass arrive in spring as water temps climb through the low 50s, with the bite improving steadily into summer. Bluefish show up not far behind and will push bait to the surface along the oceanfront ledges. Fluke move into the bay shallows and nearshore rips as summer settles in, while black sea bass stack on any hard bottom you can find. Tautog are a year-round anchor here — spring and fall are prime — and weakfish and kingfish make quieter appearances in warmer months, often overlooked but well worth targeting in the lower bay.

Read the tides carefully, because Newport's rocky points and current-swept rips are where fish feed, not open water. An outgoing tide draining the bay concentrates bait and predators alike at narrows and channel edges. For stripers and blues, cast plugs or soft plastics upgcurrent and work them through the rip. Fluke want a slow drift with bucktail tippers or Gulp-style baits along sandy transitions near structure. Tog demand patience on the rocks with green crabs fished tight to the bottom. When northeast wind muddies the oceanfront, move inside the bay where water stays cleaner and weakfish and kingfish cruise the softer bottom.
