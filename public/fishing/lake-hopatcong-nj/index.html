Lake Hopatcong, NJ fishing report

Lake Hopatcong is New Jersey's largest lake and a genuine multi-species fishery — strong populations of largemouth and smallmouth bass, walleye, hybrid striped bass, channel catfish, and panfish, with a winter ice fishery for many of the same species. Bass turn on as the shallows warm through spring and stay active all summer, walleye and hybrids feed deep and at low light, and fall brings a strong feeding window before the lake cools and the ice fishery takes over.

Largemouth hold in the weed beds, docks, and shallow cover — work soft plastics, jigs, and topwater early and late. Smallmouth relate to rocky points and drop-offs; tubes and drop-shots produce. Walleye and hybrids hold deeper and along structure, feeding hardest at dawn, dusk, and after dark on jigs, crankbaits, and live bait. Water temperature and light drive it: warming water pulls bass shallow, while the deep-water species feed best in low light and on the cooler, more stable days.
