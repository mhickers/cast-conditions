Laguna Madre, TX fishing report

Laguna Madre is Texas redfish and speckled trout country through and through — not the northeast Atlantic, so striped bass, fluke, bluefish, black sea bass, tautog, weakfish, and kingfish simply don't live here. What you're actually targeting are spotted seatrout, redfish, and flounder as the bread-and-butter species, with sheepshead stacking on structure year-round. Spring and fall bring the best trout fishing on the shallow grass flats as water temperatures sit in the sweet spot. Summer pushes fish deeper or into moving water near the ICW where oxygen levels stay higher. Winter concentrates redfish in tighter schools along deeper edges and spoil bank drop-offs.

Read the wind before anything else on Laguna Madre — sustained southerly winds push water off the flats and shut down shallow fishing fast, while northers can clear the water to gin clarity, triggering excellent sight-casting to reds. Wade the windward edges of grass flats where baitfish pile up. Weedless gold spoons and soft-plastic paddle tails on light jig heads are workhorses here. On higher tides, push skinny water for tailing reds. Low tides concentrate fish near ICW cuts and spoil island pockets — work popping corks over submerged grass or freelined live shrimp along bottom transitions.
