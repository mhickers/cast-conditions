Brigantine, NJ fishing report

Brigantine's north-end surf and the rips around Absecon Inlet make it one of the South Jersey shore's most productive stretches, especially for striped bass. The marquee event is the fall run, when bass and bluefish blitz bait along the beach from October into early winter; spring offers another shot at stripers as fish push up the coast. Through summer, fluke (summer flounder) fill the inlet and back bays, with kingfish and the occasional weakfish mixed into the suds.

In the surf, fish the moving water around the inlet and the cuts between sandbars — bucktails, swim shads, and fresh bunker or clam on a fishfinder rig all produce for stripers, while metal lures shine when blues are blitzing on top. For fluke, drift the inlet and bay channels with bucktails or a squid-and-minnow combo, working the edges where the current sweeps. Tides matter most here: the strongest bite usually comes on moving water, an hour either side of the turn, with dawn and dusk the prime windows.
