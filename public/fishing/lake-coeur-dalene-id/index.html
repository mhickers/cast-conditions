Lake Coeur d'Alene, ID fishing report

Lake Coeur d'Alene is a deep, scenic Idaho panhandle lake with a genuinely mixed bag — landlocked chinook salmon roaming the open water, strong largemouth and smallmouth bass populations, and trophy northern pike in the weedy southern bays and the connected chain lakes. Chinook fishing is best in the colder months and early season when fish hold higher in the water column; bass turn on as the shallows warm through late spring and stay active all summer; pike feed most aggressively in spring and again in fall.

For chinook, troll spoons behind flashers through open water and follow the bait and the thermocline as summer pushes fish deeper. Smallmouth relate to rocky points, drop-offs, and gravel — work tubes, drop-shots, and crankbaits along that structure. Largemouth and pike hold in the southern weed beds and the Coeur d'Alene River chain, where spinnerbaits, swimbaits, and large soft plastics around the salad draw strikes. Water temperature is the key dial here: it sets how deep the salmon suspend and when the bass and pike slide shallow to ambush.
