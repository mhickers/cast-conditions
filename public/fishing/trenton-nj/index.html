Trenton, NJ fishing report

Trenton sits at the fall line of the Delaware River, where tidal water below meets free-flowing river above, and that mix gives it a genuinely diverse fishery. Smallmouth bass thrive in the rocky, current-swept water, channel catfish prowl the deeper holes, and the river holds striped bass that run up from the tidal reaches in spring, along with white perch, walleye, and panfish. Spring and fall are the strongest seasons as fish move and feed actively in moderate water temperatures.

Work the rocky current seams, eddies, and drop-offs for smallmouth with tubes, soft plastics, and crankbaits, fishing the slack water beside fast current where bass ambush bait. Catfish hold in the deeper holes — fish cut bait on the bottom. For stripers, target the deeper runs in spring with swim shads and bucktails. Water temperature and flow are the dials: a stable, moderate flow with warming water turns the bite on, while high muddy water after rain pushes fish to slower, softer edges.
