Fort Myers, FL fishing report

The Caloosahatchee and Pine Island Sound are snook, redfish, and tarpon country at their core. Snook stack around bridges, passes, and mangrove edges through the warm months, then push into deeper river holes when water temperatures drop below the mid-60s. Redfish are resident year-round, schooling on the shallow flats in fall before scattering to oyster bars and creek mouths in winter. Spotted seatrout follow a similar rhythm, running the grass flats hard in cooler months. Tarpon move through the Sound in spring and early summer. Note that the species listed like striped bass, fluke, bluefish, tautog, weakfish, and kingfish are northeastern and mid-Atlantic fish and do not naturally occur in this Southwest Florida fishery.

For snook and reds, work the outgoing tide hard along mangrove shorelines with DOA shrimp, weedless paddle tails, or live pinfish. Low light periods at dawn and dusk consistently outperform midday. Trout favor deeper grass flats on cooler, calm mornings — a slow-sinking twitchbait or popping cork over live shrimp gets bites. When wind lays down and water clarity improves, sight-fishing shallow shell bars for redfish with a gold spoon or weedless jig is extremely effective. Avoid fishing grass flats when water temps dip below 60, as fish become lethargic and move to deeper, slower current.
