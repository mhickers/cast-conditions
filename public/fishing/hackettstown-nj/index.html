Hackettstown, NJ fishing report

Hackettstown fishes the Musconetcong River, a cold, clear trout stream that's a New Jersey classic, along with stocked and holdover trout in its runs and pools. Trout are the headline, feeding hardest in the cooler months and through spring when flows are good and water temperatures stay low; the river also holds smallmouth bass in the warmer lower stretches and panfish along the slower edges. Summer heat slows the trout bite, which rebounds in fall as the water cools.

Work the riffles, runs, and pool heads for trout — drift nymphs and small streamers through the seams, or fish small spinners and spoons through the holding water. Approach carefully in the clear, low water and fish the broken surface where trout feel safe. Smallmouth hold in the rockier lower-river current. The river is all about flow and temperature: cool water and a good moderate flow have trout active and feeding, while warm summer water and low flow push them to the coolest, most oxygenated spots.
