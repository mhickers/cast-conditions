San Diego, CA fishing report

San Diego's kelp beds and offshore banks fish differently depending on time of year, and knowing the rhythm matters. Calico bass are a year-round staple holding tight to the kelp canopy, with their best topwater action in warmer months. Yellowtail push inshore to the kelp edges in late spring through fall, following bait schools and warmer blue water. When the current pushes warm water north from Baja, bluefin and albacore tuna show up on the offshore banks anywhere from late spring into autumn. Rockfish are available year-round on the deeper rocky structure, and they hold steady when surface conditions make everything else difficult.

For yellowtail in the kelp, live sardines or mackerel fly-lined without a sinker is hard to beat, but iron jigs worked fast near the surface trigger aggressive strikes when fish are actively feeding. Calico bass respond well to swimbait presentations worked slowly through the canopy edges, especially during incoming tides when baitfish push into the kelp. Tuna offshore want a fast troll or surface iron in blue water with good current, and a sharp temperature break between cooler green and warmer blue water is exactly where to focus. Calm mornings with light wind let you read the kelp lines and spot bird activity, which often marks the fish before you mark them on sonar.
